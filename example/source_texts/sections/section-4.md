## The Map That Changes the Territory

Some maps do not describe a place so much as help to make it. A property survey records where one owner’s land ends, and once the boundary is recorded, fences follow the line and disputes are settled by it. A flood map marks the low ground as risky, and the marking can change what the land is worth and who is willing to build there.

Here the order of the usual story reverses. The territory does not simply come first and the map after: the drawn line returns to the ground in fences, in insurance premiums and in what people think of as theirs. A map of this kind has to be read as an event as well as a picture.

That leaves the reader with two questions where there was one. What was left out of this map, and for whom? And what has this map already done to the place it shows?
